PM2.5 (Fine Particulate Matter) Guidelines
PM2.5 refers to atmospheric particulate matter that has a diameter of less than 2.5 micrometers. These particles are so small they can only be detected with an electron microscope. 
Sources include combustion of fuels (such as motor vehicles, power plants, and residential wood burning), agricultural burning, and some industrial processes.
Because of their microscopic size, PM2.5 particles can penetrate deep into the respiratory tract and reach the lungs, potentially entering the bloodstream.
Typical interpretation values (general):
- < 12 µg/m³: Good
- 12.1 to 35.4 µg/m³: Moderate
- 35.5 to 55.4 µg/m³: Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups
- 55.5 to 150.4 µg/m³: Unhealthy
- 150.5 to 250.4 µg/m³: Very Unhealthy
- > 250.5 µg/m³: Hazardous
Elevated PM2.5 is a primary driver of poor air quality in urban and industrial environments.
